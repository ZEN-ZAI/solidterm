# ADR-0007 — SolidTerm is licensed GPL-3.0-or-later

Status: accepted

## Context

The GitHub repository is public and carried no `LICENSE` file, which under
copyright law means the opposite of what a public repository looks like:
readable by anyone, usable by no one. `README.md` said "None (private
development)" and `CONTRIBUTING.md` deferred the choice. Cargo manifests
carried no `license` field and `scripts/check-license-headers.sh` was a stub
waiting on this decision.

The stated intent was "anyone may use and modify it; nobody may take it and
sell it as their own closed product". Permissive licenses (MIT, Apache-2.0)
express the first half and cannot express the second. Source-available licenses
(PolyForm Noncommercial, FSL) express the second but are not open source, and
PolyForm Noncommercial would forbid a developer running SolidTerm at work.

Every dependency is permissive — MIT / Apache-2.0 / BSD / ISC / Zlib / Unicode /
Unlicense — so nothing in the graph constrains the choice upward.
`alacritty_terminal`, the largest of them, is Apache-2.0 and ships
`LICENSE-APACHE` with no `NOTICE` file.

## Decision

GPL-3.0-or-later for the whole repository: app, crates, scripts and themes.

- `LICENSE` holds the verbatim GPL-3.0 text; `[workspace.package]` carries
  `license = "GPL-3.0-or-later"` and both crates inherit it. `publish = false`
  stays — the crates are workspace-internal, not crates.io products.
- Every `.swift`, `.rs`, `.metal` and shell source outside
  `app/SolidTerm/Generated/` opens with a two-line SPDX header
  (`SPDX-License-Identifier: GPL-3.0-or-later` plus the copyright line),
  enforced in CI by `scripts/check-license-headers.sh`. The generated
  swift-bridge shims are excluded because `build-rust.sh` rewrites them on
  every build. Two hand-written files fall outside that extension set and
  carry no header: `app/SolidTerm/SolidTerm-Bridging-Header.h` and
  `.githooks/pre-commit`.
- Bundled theme TOMLs are data, not source: each opens with a palette
  attribution comment naming its upstream and license instead of an SPDX
  header.
- `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` is generated by `scripts/gen-third-party-notices.sh`
  from the crates actually linked into the binary, plus the swift-bridge
  runtime and the theme palettes. It is committed, bundled into
  `SolidTerm.app/Contents/Resources/`, linked from the About panel, and CI
  fails when the committed copy drifts from the generator's output.
- Inbound contributions are GPL-3.0-or-later. `CONTRIBUTING.md` keeps the
  clause granting the maintainer the right to relicense, so a future move
  remains possible without chasing contributors. No DCO, no CLA — the friction
  is not worth it at this size.

## Consequences

- Apache-2.0 dependencies are compatible with GPLv3 but not with GPLv2. The
  license line is therefore "or-later" from v3, never v2, and a dependency that
  is GPLv2-only cannot be added.
- `solidterm-engine` and `solidterm-ffi` are reusable by other projects only
  under the GPL. That is a real cost of the choice, accepted deliberately: the
  engine is not a library product, and a permissive engine would hand back the
  proprietary-fork hole the license exists to close.
- Redistribution through the Mac App Store by a third party is effectively
  excluded, because the App Store terms conflict with GPL section 6. Direct DMG
  distribution (ADR-0002) is unaffected.
- Selling SolidTerm is legal — with complete corresponding source under the
  same license. That is what makes a closed resale worthless rather than
  forbidden, which is the practical form of the intent.
- The binary must ship the notices it inherits. That is why
  `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` is a bundled resource and a CI gate rather than a
  file someone remembers to update.
- The name is not covered here. Trademark, not copyright, is the lever that
  protects "SolidTerm" as a name, and it is a separate decision.
